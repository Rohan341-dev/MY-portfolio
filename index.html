01 / About

A little about me.

Rohan Yadav — at work
"I started with curiosity.
Now I build with purpose."

I didn't start with a plan. I started with a browser, a broken HTML page, and the question: "How does this actually work?"

That question led me down a path I never fully mapped out — from writing my first lines of HTML and CSS, to building full-stack applications, exploring AI and large language models, launching SaaS products, and experimenting with systems that solve real problems.

I'm Rohan Yadav, publicly known as Rohan Codekage. I'm a student programmer and full-stack developer based in Nepal. I'm still young, still learning, and still very much at the beginning — but I've already built things I'm genuinely proud of.

My journey has taken me from basic frontend work into backend systems, databases, APIs, full-stack development, SaaS architecture, AI/LLM experimentation, automation, and product development. I've built management systems, e-commerce platforms, AI tools, and experimental hardware projects.

My philosophy is simple:

"Don't just learn technology. Build something with it."

I enjoy the entire process — taking an idea from Idea → Research → Design → Code → Debug → Improve → Ship. Every project teaches me something new, and every failure becomes the foundation for the next attempt.

My interests extend beyond traditional software development. I'm curious about AI and how language models can be integrated into real products. I experiment with computer vision, automation, SaaS architecture, EdTech, e-commerce, hardware like Arduino, and physics-inspired systems. I'm drawn to problems that sit at the intersection of technology and the real world.

I don't just want to learn technology. I want to build with it. And I want to build things that matter.

But somewhere along the way, I realized something. The best code in the world doesn't matter if it doesn't reach the people who need it. That realization pushed me beyond writing software — into building teams, companies, and products that solve real problems.

Today, I'm the Founder of SajiloCode Pvt. Ltd. and CEO & Founder of Code Nest Nepal Pvt. Ltd. — two ventures born from the same curiosity that first led me to a browser and a broken HTML page. I'm learning that building a company is its own form of engineering — one that requires not just code, but vision, leadership, and the courage to build something from nothing.

13 / Leadership

Leadership, according to a builder.

01

Build with people, not just for people.

The best products aren't built in isolation. They're built through collaboration, trust, and shared purpose. Leadership means creating the environment where great work can happen.

02

Give ideas room to become products.

Every idea deserves the chance to be explored. Not every idea will succeed, but the process of exploring them teaches you more than any textbook ever could.

03

Learn from mistakes instead of hiding them.

Mistakes are data. They tell you what doesn't work, which brings you closer to what does. Transparency about failure builds stronger teams and better products.

04

A company is more than its software.

It's the people, systems, culture, and vision behind it. Technology is the tool. The company is the vehicle. The team is the engine.

05

Leadership is another form of building.

Just as code is built line by line, leadership is built decision by decision, conversation by conversation, and relationship by relationship. It's engineering applied to people and organizations.
